## Mapping

* exam `e` -> pyDCOP variable `x_e`, domain = slots `0..S-1`; `g[e]` -> unary extensional constraint `g_e`;
* pair `(e, f)` with `c` students -> binary extensional constraint `n_e_f` whose table is generated by calling `vmgm.model.pc(a, b, c)`;
* an exam that shares no students gets an all-zero constraint `link_e_f` with the next exam of its department (it changes no cost; it makes pyDCOP treat the exam like any other, see README.md);
* department `i` -> pyDCOP agent `d<i>`, hosting the computations of its own 4 exams (distribution file `instances/<name>_dist.yaml`);
* objective `min`; every variable starts at our random start `M.random_start(inst, 1000 + seed)`.

pyDCOP's MGM runs one computation per **variable**: a variable moves when its gain beats the gains of all variables it shares a constraint with. Its local cost for exam `e` is `g_e` plus every binary term containing `e`, so its gain for a move of `e` is exactly the drop of our `F_cost` for that single-exam move. A pyDCOP fixed point (no variable can lower its local cost) is therefore the same as our local optimum (for every department `i`, `best_move(inst, x, i, g)[1] is None`: no single-exam move lowers `F`). The two algorithms differ in who moves in a round (pyDCOP: any number of non-adjacent exams, possibly several of one department; ours: at most one exam per department, departments compared with neighbouring departments), so their runs differ, but not what they stop at.

## Check 2: pyDCOP MGM's final assignment is a local optimum of our model

| pyDCOP MGM | (a) reported cost == `F_cost` == `solution_cost` | (b) no improving single-exam move | departments with an improving move (total) |
|---|---|---|---|
| stock | 20 / 20 | 13 / 20 | 9 |
| fixed | 20 / 20 | 20 / 20 | 0 |

The stock failures come from pyDCOP's MGM, not from the cost model (check 1 and 2(a) agree exactly): a variable computes its current local cost only in its first cycle and afterwards only subtracts its own gains, so when a neighbour moves, the cost it compares against is stale and an improving move can be missed (the stock run stops at cycle 500 although improving moves are left). `pydcop_fixed.py` recomputes the cost at the start of every cycle and changes nothing else; see README.md.

* pyDCOP MGM (stock): mean final cost **357.90**; paired difference (pyDCOP - ours) mean **14.15**, sd 37.98, min -60, max 108; pyDCOP lower on 8, equal on 2, higher on 10 of 20.
* pyDCOP MGM (fixed): mean final cost **345.50**; paired difference (pyDCOP - ours) mean **1.75**, sd 21.75, min -50, max 49; pyDCOP lower on 5, equal on 9, higher on 6 of 20.

Our MGM and the fixed pyDCOP MGM both end at local optima of the same cost function from the same start, but the runs take different paths (different movers per round, random tie-breaking among equally good slots in pyDCOP, thread timing), so the end points differ, and a re-run of pyDCOP can give slightly different values. We claim only how close the means are, not that the runs are the same.
